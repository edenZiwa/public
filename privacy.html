1. Introduction

Garden of Mercy Foundation respects the privacy, dignity and rights of individuals whose information may be entrusted to us.

This Privacy Policy explains the principles we intend to apply when collecting, using, storing, protecting and sharing personal information through our website, programs, communications, fundraising activities and other authorized organizational activities.

The Foundation's work is particularly focused on children and communities. We therefore recognize that information relating to children, families, beneficiaries and vulnerable persons requires additional care and protection.

2. Information We May Collect

Depending on the services and activities available, we may collect information such as:

  • name and preferred form of address;
  • email address;
  • telephone or mobile number;
  • postal or physical address where necessary;
  • organization or professional information;
  • donation and transaction information;
  • volunteer or application information;
  • messages and correspondence;
  • information submitted through forms;
  • photographs, videos or other media where appropriately authorized;
  • website usage and technical information;
  • information required to administer programs;
  • information required to meet legal or regulatory obligations.

We seek to collect only information that is relevant and reasonably necessary for the particular purpose for which it is obtained.

3. Children, Beneficiaries and Vulnerable Persons

Because Garden of Mercy Foundation is child-focused, protecting children and vulnerable people is a central consideration in our information practices.

Information relating to children should only be collected, accessed, used or disclosed for legitimate organizational purposes and with appropriate authorization and safeguards.

Child protection principle

The interests, dignity, safety and privacy of children should take priority over publicity, fundraising or organizational convenience.

Where consent is required for processing a child's personal information, the Foundation should obtain appropriate consent from a parent, guardian or other legally authorized person, subject to applicable law and safeguarding requirements.

Sensitive information about children should not be publicly displayed unless there is a lawful, documented and appropriately authorized reason to do so.

4. How We Use Information

Personal information may be used for legitimate organizational purposes, including:

  • responding to enquiries;
  • communicating with donors and supporters;
  • administering donations;
  • managing volunteers and partners;
  • delivering and monitoring programs;
  • supporting beneficiaries;
  • maintaining organizational records;
  • improving website functionality;
  • preventing fraud and abuse;
  • maintaining information security;
  • meeting legal and regulatory obligations;
  • producing appropriately aggregated impact reports;
  • communicating approved organizational news and updates.

We should not use personal information for purposes that are materially incompatible with the purpose for which it was originally collected unless permitted or required by applicable law.

5. Legal Basis for Processing

Where applicable, the Foundation will seek an appropriate legal basis for processing personal information.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

  • consent;
  • performance of a requested service or agreement;
  • compliance with a legal obligation;
  • protection of vital interests;
  • legitimate organizational interests where permitted by law.

Where processing is based on consent, individuals may generally withdraw that consent subject to applicable legal limitations and circumstances where another lawful basis applies.

6. Information Sharing and Disclosure

Garden of Mercy Foundation does not intend to sell personal information.

Information may be shared where reasonably necessary with:

  • authorized Foundation personnel;
  • service providers supporting Foundation operations;
  • payment and financial service providers;
  • technology, hosting and security providers;
  • professional advisers where appropriate;
  • authorized program partners;
  • government or regulatory authorities where legally required;
  • law-enforcement authorities where legally required or permitted.

Access should be limited according to the person's role and the principle of least privilege.

7. Donations and Payment Information

When donation functionality is enabled, payment transactions may be processed through authorized payment providers.

Where possible, sensitive payment credentials should be handled directly by the authorized payment provider rather than unnecessarily stored by the Foundation.

Donation records may include information necessary to verify, reconcile, acknowledge and account for a donation.

Payment security

The Foundation should never request passwords, PINs, one-time authentication codes or full payment credentials through ordinary email, social media messages or unofficial communication channels.

8. Photography, Video and Communications

As an NGO, the Foundation may document activities, events and programs through photographs, video, written stories or other media.

Particular care should be taken when media involves children, beneficiaries or vulnerable individuals.

Where consent or authorization is required, appropriate authorization should be obtained before publication.

Public communications should avoid unnecessary disclosure of identifying, sensitive or potentially harmful information.

Stories should preserve the dignity of beneficiaries and should not intentionally portray people in a degrading, exploitative or misleading manner.

9. Safeguarding and Protection from Harm

Garden of Mercy Foundation is committed to maintaining an environment in which children and vulnerable people are treated with dignity and protected from abuse, exploitation, harassment and other forms of harm.

Information relating to safeguarding concerns may be handled with heightened confidentiality and restricted access.

Confidentiality does not prevent the Foundation from reporting or sharing information where disclosure is necessary to protect a person from harm or comply with applicable law.

10. Cookies and Similar Technologies

The website may use cookies or similar technologies for purposes such as:

  • essential website functionality;
  • security;
  • session management;
  • preferences;
  • analytics where enabled;
  • performance monitoring.

Where legally required, appropriate consent or controls should be provided for non-essential cookies.

11. Information Security

We seek to apply reasonable technical, organizational and administrative safeguards to protect personal information.

Security measures may include:

  • role-based access controls;
  • strong authentication requirements;
  • secure password storage;
  • encryption where appropriate;
  • secure communications;
  • security monitoring;
  • backups and recovery procedures;
  • audit logging;
  • staff and administrator access controls;
  • security updates and vulnerability management.

No internet-based system can be guaranteed to be completely secure. We therefore encourage users to protect their own accounts and devices appropriately.

12. Data Retention

Personal information should only be retained for as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, legitimate organizational requirements, safeguarding, accounting, legal, regulatory or dispute-resolution purposes.

When information is no longer required, it should be securely deleted, anonymized or otherwise disposed of according to applicable retention procedures.

13. Your Privacy Rights

Subject to applicable law, individuals may have rights relating to their personal information, including:

  • the right to request access to personal information;
  • the right to request correction of inaccurate information;
  • the right to request deletion where legally applicable;
  • the right to object to certain processing;
  • the right to withdraw consent where processing relies on consent;
  • the right to raise a privacy complaint.

Requests may be subject to identity verification and applicable legal exceptions.

14. Personal Data Incidents and Breaches

If the Foundation becomes aware of a suspected unauthorized access, loss, disclosure, alteration or destruction of personal information, it should investigate and take reasonable steps to contain and address the incident.

Where notification is required by applicable law, relevant authorities and affected individuals should be notified in accordance with the applicable requirements.

15. International Data Transfers

Some technology, hosting, communication, payment or other service providers may process information outside Uganda.

Where personal information is transferred across borders, the Foundation should take appropriate steps to ensure that the transfer and subsequent processing comply with applicable privacy and data-protection requirements.

16. Third-Party Websites and Services

Our website may contain links to third-party websites, payment platforms, social-media services or other external systems.

Those services operate under their own privacy policies and terms. Garden of Mercy Foundation is not responsible for privacy practices outside our reasonable control.

17. Volunteers, Staff and Partners

Information relating to employees, volunteers, consultants and partners may be collected when necessary to administer their relationship with the Foundation.

Access to such information should be restricted to authorized personnel and used only for legitimate organizational purposes.

18. Donor and Supporter Communications

Supporters may receive communications relating to donations, organizational activities, campaigns, events or other legitimate Foundation activities.

Where applicable, recipients should be provided with an appropriate mechanism to opt out of non-essential promotional communications.

19. Transparency and Accountability

Garden of Mercy Foundation seeks to promote responsible stewardship of information and resources.

Access to sensitive information should be based on legitimate responsibilities, documented procedures and appropriate authorization.

Where appropriate, organizational activities should maintain records that support accountability, transparency and responsible governance.

20. Changes to This Policy

This Privacy Policy may be updated when our services, technology, organizational practices or applicable legal requirements change.

The latest version should be published on this page together with an updated effective or revision date.

21. Privacy Questions and Complaints

Questions, requests or complaints relating to privacy and personal information may be submitted through our contact page.
